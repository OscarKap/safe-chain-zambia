# Digital Safety & Privacy

## Small steps, big protection

You don't need to be a tech expert to stay safer online. Start with what feels manageable — and if someone may be watching your phone, make changes carefully, because sudden changes can sometimes alert an abuser.

## Protect your accounts

- Use a different strong password or PIN for your phone, email, social media and mobile money
- Turn on two-step verification (a code sent by SMS or app)
- Never share passwords or one-time codes — not even with a partner
- Log out of accounts on shared or borrowed phones

## Check your phone

- Review which apps can see your location, camera and microphone
- Look for apps you don't remember installing
- Check "logged-in devices" in WhatsApp, Facebook, Google and other accounts and remove ones you don't recognise
- Turn off location sharing you didn't choose

## Social media privacy

- Set profiles to private and limit who can message you
- Think before posting your school, home area, or live location
- Block and report accounts that harass you

## Protecting children and young people online

Children and teenagers can be targeted through games, social media and chat apps.

- Talk openly and calmly about what they do online, without blame
- Teach them never to send photos or meet someone they only know online without a trusted adult
- Explain that anyone asking for secrets, photos or money is a warning sign
- Make sure they know they can tell you if something goes wrong — and that they won't be punished for being targeted

If a child is being exploited or abused online, report it. Child protection services and the Victim Support Unit can help.

## If your device may be monitored

Use a trusted device — a friend's phone, a library or school computer — to look for help, change passwords or contact a responder.
