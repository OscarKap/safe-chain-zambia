# Mental Wellbeing

Mental wellbeing is about how we think, feel, cope with life, and relate to others.

Just like physical health, mental health matters.

Many young people silently struggle with:

- Stress
- Depression
- Anxiety
- Loneliness
- Family pressure
- Relationship problems
- School pressure
- Financial stress
- Social media pressure
- Grief and heartbreak

In many African communities, mental health is often ignored or misunderstood. Some people are told to “be strong,” “pray harder,” or “keep quiet.” But emotional pain is real, and support matters.

You do not have to suffer alone.

## Stress

Stress happens when life feels overwhelming.

Young people may feel stressed because of:

- School or exams
- Unemployment
- Family conflict
- Money problems
- Relationship issues
- Peer pressure
- Social media comparisons

A little stress is normal. Too much stress can affect health and daily life.

## Signs of Too Much Stress

You may notice:

- Constant worry
- Trouble sleeping
- Headaches
- Feeling angry easily
- Tiredness
- Difficulty concentrating
- Loss of motivation
- Changes in eating habits
- Anxiety

Anxiety is more than ordinary worry.

It can feel like:

- Constant fear
- Racing thoughts
- Panic
- Tight chest
- Fast heartbeat
- Feeling unsafe even when nothing is happening

Some young people experience anxiety quietly for years without understanding what is happening.

## Depression

Depression is not laziness or weakness.

It is a serious emotional condition that can affect:

- Energy
- Mood
- Sleep
- Appetite
- Motivation
- Hope for the future

Someone with depression may:

- Feel numb or hopeless
- Lose interest in life
- Withdraw from others
- Cry often
- Feel worthless
- Struggle to get out of bed
- Heartbreak and Emotional Pain

Breakups and rejection can deeply affect mental health, especially for young people.

Heartbreak can cause:

- Sadness
- Loss of confidence
- Isolation
- Anxiety
- Depression

Sometimes people feel embarrassed talking about emotional pain because others may dismiss it.

But emotional pain is real.

Healing takes time.

## Suicide Thoughts

Sometimes emotional pain becomes so heavy that a person starts thinking:

- “I want to disappear.”
- “People would be better without me.”
- “I can’t continue anymore.”

These thoughts should always be taken seriously.

Having suicidal thoughts does NOT mean someone is weak or crazy. It often means they are overwhelmed and need support.

## Warning Signs Someone Needs Immediate Help

A person may:

- Talk about death often
- Withdraw completely
- Give away belongings
- Say goodbye unusually
- Self-harm
- Lose hope completely
- Abuse alcohol or drugs heavily

If you notice these signs, do not ignore them.

### What To Do If You Feel Overwhelmed
- Talk to Someone

You do not have to carry everything alone.

Speak to:

- A trusted friend
- Parent or guardian
- Teacher
- Counselor
- Religious leader you trust
- Health worker
- Rest Your Mind

Sometimes the body and mind need recovery.

Try:

- Sleeping properly
- Reducing constant phone stress
- Spending time outside
- Taking breaks from pressure
- Avoid Harmful Coping

Some people try to escape pain through:

- Alcohol
- Drugs
- Dangerous behavior
- Isolation

These may increase problems later.

## Write or Express Yourself

Journaling, music, art, prayer, or talking can help release emotional pressure.

## Mental Health and Young Men

Many boys and young men are taught:

- “Men don’t cry.”
- “Be tough.”
- “Handle it alone.”

This silence can become dangerous.

Men also deserve emotional support.

## Mental Health and Young Women

Girls and young women may face:

- Relationship pressure
- GBV
- Body shaming
- School stress
- Family expectations
- Economic pressure

These experiences can affect confidence and emotional wellbeing deeply.

## Social Media and Mental Health

Social media can sometimes increase:

- Anxiety
- Comparison
- Loneliness
- Pressure to look perfect

- Remember:
People usually post their best moments, not their struggles.

Your value is not measured by likes or followers.

## Supporting a Friend

If a friend is struggling:

- Listen without judging
- Check in regularly
- Encourage them to seek help
- Take suicide comments seriously

Avoid saying:

- “Just be strong.”
- “Others have it worse.”
- “You’re overreacting.”

Sometimes simply listening helps more than trying to “fix” everything.

## Youth Counselling Support

Counselling gives young people a safe space to:

- Talk openly
- Process emotions
- Learn coping skills
- Heal from trauma

Support may be available through:

- Clinics
- Schools
- Youth centers
- NGOs
- Churches or faith groups
- Mental health professionals
- GBV support organizations

Some services are free or low-cost.

## When To Seek Professional Help

Seek support if:

- Sadness lasts many weeks
- Anxiety affects daily life
- You cannot cope normally
- You think about self-harm
- Trauma or abuse is affecting you
- You feel unsafe emotionally

Asking for help is a sign of strength, not failure.

## Important Reminder

Your mental health matters just as much as your physical health.

Needing support does not make someone weak. Life can become heavy sometimes, and nobody is meant to carry everything alone.
