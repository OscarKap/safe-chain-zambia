# Consent & Boundaries

## Consent & Boundaries

Consent means giving clear, willing permission for something to happen — especially in relationships and sexual situations.

It is about respect, safety, choice, and dignity.

Every person has the right to decide:

- What happens to their body
- Who touches them
- What kind of relationship they want
- When to say yes
- When to say no

Consent is not only about sex. It also applies to:

- Touching
- Kissing
- Sharing photos
- Online conversations
- Emotional pressure
- Personal space
- What Real Consent Looks Like

Consent should be:

- Clear

A person clearly agrees.

Examples:

- “Yes”
- “I want to”
- “I’m comfortable with this”

Silence is NOT consent.

## Freely Given

The person is not pressured, threatened, manipulated, or forced.

Consent is not real if someone is:

- Afraid
- Drunk or unconscious
- Too young to legally agree
- Threatened financially or emotionally
- Ongoing

Someone can change their mind at any time.

Even if a person agreed before, they can still say:

- “Stop”
- “Not anymore”
- “I’m uncomfortable”

And that decision must be respected immediately.

## Mutual

Healthy relationships involve listening, respect, and comfort from both people.

## What Consent Is NOT

Consent is NOT:

- Fear
- Silence
- Pressure
- Manipulation
- Begging until someone gives up
- Threats
- Forced marriage
- Exchange for money, grades, jobs, or gifts
- Assuming because you are dating or married

Being in a relationship does not remove someone’s right to say no.

Even married partners must respect consent.

## Understanding Boundaries

Boundaries are personal limits people set to protect:

- Their body
- Emotions
- Privacy
- Comfort
- Safety

Everyone’s boundaries are different.

Examples:

- “I don’t want to be touched.”
- “I’m not ready for sex.”
- “Don’t share my photos.”
- “I need privacy.”
- “I don’t like that joke.”

Healthy people respect boundaries without anger or punishment.

## Consent in African Communities

In many communities, young people — especially girls — are taught to stay silent or obey older partners without question.

Some harmful beliefs include:

- “A husband cannot be refused.”
- “Men should always control relationships.”
- “Girls who say no are disrespectful.”
- “Buying gifts means someone owes sex.”

These beliefs can lead to abuse and exploitation.

Respecting consent protects everyone.

## Digital Consent

Consent also matters online.

You should never:

- Share someone’s private photos without permission
- Pressure someone to send intimate pictures
- Read private messages without permission
- Record someone secretly
- Post someone’s information to embarrass them

Digital abuse is real harm.

## Warning Signs of Unhealthy Relationships

A relationship may be unsafe if someone:

- Controls your movements
- Checks your phone constantly
- Threatens you
- Pressures you sexually
- Gets angry when you say no
- Isolates you from friends or family
- Uses gifts or money to control you
- Hits, insults, or humiliates you

Love should not feel like fear.

## Peer Pressure and Young People

Many teenagers feel pressure from:

- Friends
- Social media
- Older partners
- School environments

You never have to do something sexual just to:

- Fit in
- Keep a relationship
- Receive money or gifts
- Avoid being mocked

Saying no is your right.

## If Someone Violates Your Consent

If someone forces, pressures, or harms you:

- It is not your fault
- You deserve support
- You have the right to seek help

You can speak to:

- A trusted adult
- Counselor
- Health worker
- Teacher
- Social worker
- Police Victim Support Unit
- GBV support organization
- Supporting Survivors

If someone shares an experience of abuse:

- Listen calmly
- Do not blame them
- Believe them
- Help them find support

Statements like:

- “Why were you there?”
- “What were you wearing?”
- “You should have known better.”

…can cause more harm.

## Consent and Disabilities

People with disabilities also have full rights to:

- Relationships
- Privacy
- Safety
- Consent

No one should assume a person with a disability cannot make decisions or deserves less protection.

- Healthy Relationships Should Include
- Respect
- Communication
- Trust
- Safety
- Honesty
- Equal decision-making
- Freedom to say no

A person who truly cares about you will respect your boundaries.

## Important Reminder

“No” is enough.

And consent is not just about avoiding harm — it’s about building relationships based on dignity, trust, and mutual respect.
